    What this is
    
      
    The lab station for Project 1 in your browser: a Niryo One, the platform with its four markers, squares A, B and C, foam cubes and the camera. The project's Python programs run here unchanged, in the browser, with the same windows, keys and robot commands as in the lab.
    
      
    Running a program
    
      
      
        
    1. Choose the program at the top (they are in step order), add options if needed (for example --dry-run) and press Run.
      2. 
        
    2. Windows open on top of the page. Click a camera picture before pressing a key: as in the lab, it only receives keys when selected.
      3. 
        
    3. Stop works like Ctrl+C. Restart Python is for a program that does not stop.
      4. 
      
    
      
    Editing your code
    
      
    Open Files and editor, choose a file, edit it and press Ctrl+S. Your files, dataset, model and results are kept in this browser. Use Download project to keep a copy or to move it to a lab PC.
    
      
    The station
    
      
      
        
    • Drag the view to turn it, right-drag to pan, wheel to zoom.
      • 
        
    • Drag a cube to move it, use the wheel over a cube to turn it, double-click it to remove it.
      • 
        
    • Learning mode (steps 1.1 and 1.4): drag on the platform to move the gripper; click the 3D view, then use the arrow keys (Shift = 5 mm), PgUp and PgDn for height, and [ ] to turn.
      • 
        
    • The camera behaves like the lab's: automatic exposure and colour change when a hand or the arm is in view, and the focus hunts until step 1.3 locks it.
      • 
        
    • Use Desk lamp for the lighting test in step 3 (collect it with --dataset dataset_lighting2).
      • 
      
    
      
    Differences from the lab
    
      
      
        
    • Choose the robot Simulator in step 1.1; its Lab 5 positions can be loaded.
      • 
        
    • Voice commands use the browser's speech recognition (Chrome or Edge), or words typed in the voice box.
      • 
        
    • The simulated pictures are cleaner than the real camera: expect higher accuracy here than in the lab. Final results for your report come from the real station.
